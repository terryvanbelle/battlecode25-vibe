# The objective, restated (2026-09-10)

**Binding. Read after your charter and before TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md.**

## What changed

Until today both live lineages optimised the **head-to-head gap** — "what does the
lineage beating me do that I don't." Both drove that framing to exhaustion on the
same afternoon: complete mechanism enumerations, every design premise closed, and
each one's surviving premise refuted at its funding half. Both wrote closure maps.
Neither manufactured an extra axis, which was the right call.

The framing is what was exhausted, not the game. **The objective is now absolute
strength.**

## Why the old framing had to end

From `benchmarks/HISTORY.md`, committed:

| lineage | vs `v3` | vs `TSPAARKHS` |
|---|---|---|
| alice | 17.3% | ~1.3% |
| carol | 24.7% | 0.0% |

**Both lineages lose three quarters to all of their games against the external
standard, and have spent weeks contesting a three-point gap between each other.**
A joint local optimum is exactly what that produces: two bots co-adapted to each
other's specific weaknesses, each correctly concluding that the other's axes are
closed.

## The new objective

1. **Maximise absolute strength.** Not the deficit against the other lineage.
2. **Primary outcome instrument: your frozen roster**, which both of you extended
   with cross-architecture rungs today. That is what answers "am I getting
   stronger" without a co-evolving reference.
3. **External validation: the committed benchmark score.** You may read
   `benchmarks/HISTORY.md`. Everything else about those bots is unchanged and
   hard — never their code, never a replay, never run those matches, never add
   one as an opponent to anything. The chain that produces the score is
   coordinator-run. It is a yardstick, and reading a number off it is the whole
   of your access.
4. **The organising question changes** from *"what does the rival do that I
   don't"* to *"what limits my own absolute performance."*
5. **The tournament keeps running and stays useful** — it is still the only
   measurement against opponents you did not produce, and still your best source
   of diagnostics. What changes is that a head-to-head margin is now a
   diagnostic, not the goal.

## What this does to your closure maps

Keep them. They are records and most of their entries transfer. But:

> **A closure established against one objective does not transfer to another
> automatically. Re-examine it; do not re-run it.**

For every entry, ask one question: **was its closing number computed relative to
the rival, or absolutely?**

- **Transfers unchanged:** engine facts, structural impossibilities, oracle
  ceilings computed against your own bar, phase relationships, gate
  un-implementability, anything measured on your own bot alone.
- **Does NOT transfer:** every closure of the form *"the winner is on the other
  side of this metric, so I am not behind."* That check killed several candidates
  in both lineages. It is a comparison against a bot that loses three quarters of
  its own games — **being ahead of the rival is not evidence of being near
  optimal**, and those directions are re-opened by the change of objective, not by
  preference.

That re-examination is cheap, it is not manufacturing an axis, and it is the first
work under the new objective. Record which entries survive and which re-open, with
the reason, in the closure map you already wrote.

## What does not change

Your bar. Your pre-registration discipline. Isolation between live lineages.
The shared-VM rules. The rewrite authorisation, which is standing and
unconditional. A rejected iteration is still a delivered result.
